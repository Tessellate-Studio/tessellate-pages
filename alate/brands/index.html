What one fit-driven return actually costs you

On a typical independent-brand order, that chain can erase the whole margin. The cheapest return is the one that never happens.

  • Shipping — both directions, usually on you
  • Inspection, repackaging, and restocking labour
  • Stock locked in transit while the season moves on
  • Refund processed; payment fees not refunded
  • A customer whose first impression is "their sizing is off"

What Alate changes

Your size chart, shown at the exact moment a shopper picks a size.

Fewer fit-driven returns

Shoppers get a fit verdict from your actual size chart before they buy — instead of guessing, ordering two sizes, and sending one back.

Customers who come back

A first order that fits builds the thing money can't buy: trust in your sizing. That's what turns a one-time buyer into a repeat customer who orders without hesitating.

More carts make it to checkout

Shoppers abandon carts when they can't tell whether the size, the cut or the colour will work on them. Alate reads all of that from your product page, so a clear "this will work on you" arrives at the size-pick moment and converts the hesitant ones.

Fewer "will this fit me?" emails

When the answer is already on the product — in your numbers, against their measurements — fewer of those support threads arrive.

Import, don't retype

Paste your size chart, import it from your own store pages, or pull it from product metafields. You review every row before it's saved.

Big-retail fit tech, indie install

Sold out isn't lost

When a shopper's size is out of stock, Alate offers to email them when it comes back — re-checked against your live store. You decide which products qualify.

A returns story worth telling

Every avoided return is two shipments and a pile of packaging that never happen. If your brand stands for slow fashion, that's not a footnote — it's marketing material.

What your shoppers see

Two places the answer shows up — your store, and their pocket.

Both are built on the same size chart you upload. Neither sends a shopper's body data to your store — only the size label comes back.

On your own product page

A "What's my size?" button appears on any product you've given a body-measurement size chart. Shoppers enter a few measurements — chest, waist, hips — or tap through from their Alate profile, and get your size label back, with a note if that size is sold out. No app needed. The button tells them their measurements are used once and never shared with your store.

What you control

Every number a shopper sees is one you entered.

  • Size chartsPer product, per collection, or one chart for the whole shop. Body measurements in cm or inches.
  • Fit offeringsTall and petite ranges, extended sizes, longer or shorter sleeves and inseams, made-to-measure — with the extra length, price difference, lead time and how to order.
  • Model heightPer product, so Alate can say where a hem lands on a shopper who isn't the model's height.
  • Who you cut forTell Alate you cut for tall, petite, plus, wide, modest or slow-fashion shoppers. It's stored with your shop today, and it's how we'll choose which brands to lead with when shopper discovery launches.
  • Restock alertsMark a product you won't restock and Alate stops offering back-in-stock notifications on it — nobody is left waiting.
  • Your data, your exitUninstall any time. Your catalog was never copied, and the button and your charts come off your store with the app.

Questions brands ask

The short answers.

Do I need a developer?

No. You install from a link we send you, upload or import your size charts in the dashboard, and switch the "What's my size?" block on in your theme editor. Nothing in your theme code changes.

What data leaves my store?

Product title, price, options and stock — read live when a shopper asks, never copied. Alate keeps only what you type in — size charts, fit options, model heights — plus the encrypted connection to your store.

What does a shopper have to share?

For the on-store button, a few body measurements they type in themselves — used once and discarded — or a tap-through from their Alate profile. Either way, your store only ever receives the resulting size label.

What if my sizing is unusual?

That's the point. Alate matches a shopper's measurements to your chart, not to a generic S/M/L — so a brand that runs small, or cuts for tall or petite bodies, finally gets recommended correctly.

What happens if I uninstall?

The button disappears from your store and Alate stops adding to your products. Your catalog, theme and checkout were never modified, so there is nothing to undo.
