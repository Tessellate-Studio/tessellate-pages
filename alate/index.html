Buying blind

the label was never the problem.

A medium in one brand is a large in the next. The guessing is what costs you.

  • Ordering two sizes because you can't tell — and paying for both until one goes back
  • The repacking, the drop-off, the refund that takes its time
  • A wardrobe of almost-fits you kept because returning felt like work
  • Sticking to the same two brands because at least you know their sizing

How it works

set up once. then every link is a fit check.

01

tell alate about your body

Eleven quick questions — which sizes you shop, height, shoulders, arms, bust, torso, legs, waist, tummy, hips and thighs — answered by tapping. No tape measure, no photos of you, no account.

asked once, at your first fit check

02

bring a link

Share a product straight from the store's app or website, paste it, or just copy it and open Alate — it spots the link and shows the product's name. Links from a brand's own app are followed to the product page.

found it at midnight? copy, open, done

03

read the fit card

The size likely to fit you, in that brand's sizes; how sure Alate is and why; where it sits on you, area by area; what the fabric will do; whether your size is in stock; and whether it's in your budget.

before you've spent anything

04

slow store? keep going

Some shops take a while to read. Alate keeps trying in the background for up to six hours and tells you in the app when your fit check is ready.

no refresh ritual

The fit card

one card, built on your body — not the model's.

Every line on the card comes from something real — the brand's chart, the product page, the fabric, or what other buyers said — and the card tells you which.

the brand's own sizes

Numbers when the brand uses numbers — EU 38, IT 42, UK 12 — letters when it uses letters. No translating a chart in your head.

Size to try

how sure, and why

A confidence word with its reason: the brand's own chart, a garment measurement, or a general standard chart. A size from a standard chart is never marked as sure.

Confidence

works for you, and watch-outs

Where it sits close, where it has room, and what to look out for — one idea per line, area by area.

Works for you

the model, and you

When the store lists the model's height and size, Alate compares them with yours — so you know the hem that hits the model's knee lands mid-calf on you.

Model vs you

what the fabric will do

Lined or not, knit or woven, bias cut, how much it stretches and springs back, how it drapes — read from the product page.

Fabric build

see-through?

A line on how sheer it's likely to be, from what buyers told us and a read of the store's own product photo.

Advanced — on request

exact numbers, when you have them.

For people who know their measurements. Ask in the app — Account → Advanced features — and once we've switched it on for your email, it works on every phone signed in with that Google account.

  • Your own numbers. Up to nine measurements in cm or inches, inside leg included — every fit check then uses them.
  • Read a tailor's sheet. Photograph a measurement sheet, crop it, check what Alate read, then save.
  • Share by a link you can stop. Pick which measurements and how long — a week up to three months. See how many times it was opened, and stop it any time from Shared links.

Your data

what alate needs, reads and keeps.

Your body profile lives on your phone. Here's everything else, in plain words.

What it needs

  • Your eleven tapped answers
  • A product link
  • That's it — no account, no name, no photos of you

What it reads

  • The one product page you gave it: name, sizes, size chart, price, stock, fabric, model details
  • The store's product photo, for colour and see-through
  • Your clipboard only when you open the fit-check panel, and only to spot a link

On your phone

  • Body profile and budget
  • Fit checks and bought pieces
  • Your shared-links list
  • A random ID made when you install

Sent, then discarded

  • Your profile, with each fit check — used for that check, then thrown away
  • The product link — read, then thrown away

Kept only when you ask

  • Your synced copy, if you sign in — until you delete the account
  • A restock alert — your email goes once it's sent
  • A shared link — until it expires or you stop it
  • A slow store's link — six hours at most
  • Usage stats, only if you switch them on — with a random ID, never your name, measurements or links

Never

  • No ads, and no data sold
  • No tracking across other apps
  • No photos of you
  • No AI writing your verdict — fit cards come from fixed rules; AI only reads product photos and pages

Questions

the short answers.

More in the guide and on support.

Which stores does it work with?

Many online clothing stores. Some can't be read yet; tap "Notify me when added" on the card and we'll tell you when one is. A few stores ask not to be read or block automated reading — for those Alate stops and sends you to the store instead.

Is it a guarantee?

No — it's the size likely to fit you, with how sure Alate is and why. A card built on the brand's own chart is surer than one built on a standard chart, and the card says which it is.

Why does it say "38" and not "M"?

Because that's how the brand sizes it. Alate answers in the brand's own sizes, so what you see is what you order.

Do I need an account?

No. Fit checks work without one. Google sign-in is optional; it syncs your profile and pieces across phones, and it's needed for Advanced features.

Shoes?

Not yet. Alate sizes clothing today.

Where can I get it?
